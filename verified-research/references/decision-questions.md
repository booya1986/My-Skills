# Decision-shaped research: the angles a "what is X" report misses

When the question behind the research is "should I take, build, buy or lead X", a
report that explains X is only half the deliverable. These are the angles that turn a
good topic report into something the user can act on. Plan them in decode, run the
ones that need evidence as lenses, and write the rest from the verified material.

| # | Angle | The question it answers | Needs new research? |
|---|-------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | The mandate behind the mandate | What does the sponsor actually mean? List the 3–4 readings of the brief, what each builds, and which the evidence favors. Add the questions to ask the sponsor before saying yes. | Yes (sponsor-priority surveys, credibility research) |
| 2 | Who wins, who loses | Every stakeholder: what they lose, what they gain, what to offer them, the warning sign. | Partly (ownership-conflict evidence) |
| 3 | The end user's point of view | Employees, managers, customers: what they want, why they do not use what exists, what would make them. | Yes |
| 4 | The alternative to doing it at all | Could the same value come from an existing team, a partner, a vendor, a federated model? When is the proposed thing right vs wrong? | Yes (operating-model evidence, reabsorption cases) |
| 5 | Cost and the promise | Year-one budget frame with sourced lines marked and unsourced lines named as gaps; the 3 promises the user will be measured on. | Yes (benchmarks, salary bands, KPI standards) |
| 6 | Personal fit | What the user brings, what they lack, what to close vs hire around, effect on their path, exit conditions. | No (analysis) |
| 7 | Pre-mortem | Three failure stories at 18 months, their early signs, what to do now. | No (built from verified evidence) |
| 8 | Who to talk to | An interview guide for the 3–4 people who hold the knowledge that is not written down. | No |
| 9 | Measurement framework | Goals, metrics, definition, how measured, target (sourced vs proposed), cadence, stage; success by year; what not to measure. | Partly (published standards and KPI trees) |

## How to use it

- **decode:** mark which angles apply and whether each needs its own lens.
- **critic:** if the question is decision-shaped and fewer than five of these are
  covered, run the missing evidence-needing ones as round two before delivering.
- **consolidate:** give them their own section ("questions the first version did not
  ask" works well) rather than scattering them through the note.

## Why this matters

After a first topic report, the natural follow-up is "are there other points of view or
questions I should add?", and the answer is almost always this list. Doing them by
default saves a round trip and produces the version that actually gets shared.
