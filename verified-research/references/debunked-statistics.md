# Debunked and untraceable statistics

A growing list of famous numbers that failed fact-checking in past runs. Verify reads
this before chasing anything; consolidate appends to it whenever a number fails.
Each entry: the claim as it circulates, what was found, and what to say instead.
Do not re-chase an entry here unless a primary source is in hand.

The seed entries below are widely circulated, globally known claims whose problems are
documented in public sources. Your own runs will add domain rows of their own.

## Learning and development

| Claim | What was found | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Only 15% of trainees apply what they learn" (the 85%/15% transfer figure) | No study, method, sample or definition retrievable; cited to a mid-2000s book without a page; circulates in incompatible forms | Argue transfer from the meta-analysis by Blume et al. 2010 (Journal of Management) |
| 70-20-10, "70% of learning happens on the job" | Origin is a small group of executives retrospectively self-reporting in the 1980s; Clardy 2018 (Human Resource Development Review) is the peer-reviewed teardown | Present 70-20-10 as a heuristic, not a measurement |

## Data and analytics

| Claim | What was found | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "80% of an analyst's / data scientist's time goes to cleaning data" | Folklore drift from a disputed general claim; no primary study with a stated method and sample | Cite a current practitioner survey with its year and sample (e.g., the annual dbt Labs state-of-analytics survey naming data quality as the top problem), labeled self-reported |
| "Gartner: poor data quality costs organizations $12.9M a year" presented as a current figure | Real, but from a 2020 survey of data-quality-software buyers, self-reported | Cite with the year and the sample, or skip |

## Technology programs and transformation

| Claim | What was found | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Gartner: 55–75% of ERP projects fail" | Not found in any Gartner document; likely conflated with Standish Group / Panorama figures that use different definitions of failure | Cite a named survey with its own definition of failure and its sample, or describe the failure modes qualitatively |
| "70% of digital transformations fail" (attributed to McKinsey or BCG) | Untraceable to a single dated primary publication with a method; repeated across decades from different, incompatible surveys | Cite a specific, dated survey with its sample and its own definition of success |
| Vendor efficiency claims ("X% faster", "Y% fewer errors") in marketing pages | Vendor-reported, no methodology, no independent measurement | State that the capability is verified and that independent outcome measurement was not found |

## How to append

Add a row under the right domain (create the domain heading if needed) with the three
columns. Keep the "say instead" column to a verified number with its source, so the next
writer has a replacement, not just a prohibition. Keep entries to claims that circulate
publicly; nothing from private or internal material.
